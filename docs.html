Verified Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Concise, protocol-accurate answers. For deeper detail, follow the linked sections.

What is Numen Network?
Numen Network is a Proof-of-Scan Layer 1 blockchain combining permissionless PoW block proposal, GRANDPA BFT finality, full EVM compatibility on Chain ID 32026, and Spectral3D 3D geometry hashing. Its thesis: scanning becomes proof, proof becomes consensus, consensus becomes a ledger for things.
What is Proof-of-Scan (PoScan)?
PoScan replaces arbitrary hash grinding with deterministic geometry. A nonce seeds a 3D asteroid mesh; 4,096 surface rays are sampled into a 23-dimension spectral signature. If the signature's hash meets the difficulty target, the block seal is valid. Verifiers re-execute the exact pipeline per seal without storing raw meshes. See the pipeline breakdown.
Which Ethereum tools work with Numen?
All standard tooling works unmodified against Chain ID 32026: MetaMask, Foundry, Hardhat, Remix, ethers.js, viem, and wagmi. Copy-paste configurations are available in the Developer Hub.
How fast are blocks and finality?
Miners propose a block roughly every 10 seconds. GRANDPA validators vote in sessions of 60 blocks, finalizing them irreversibly within about 30 seconds. Production and finality are strictly decoupled.
What do miners earn?
Each sealed block pays 16 NMN. Rewards halve every 12.5 million blocks — approximately every four years at the 10-second target.
How does difficulty stay stable?
Numen uses ASERT (Absolutely Scheduled Exponential Target), anchored to genesis rather than the previous block. Difficulty self-heals: if hashpower drops drastically, it halves every 30 minutes until the 10-second cadence resumes, eliminating oscillation and manipulation vectors.
Is mainnet live?
Not yet. Numen runs as a hardened testnet (v0.2.0) with no sudo keys. Mainnet genesis — validator bootstrap, mining pool integration, and public RPC clusters — is in development per the roadmap.
What can Spectral3D be used for?
Spectral3D converts rigid geometry into stable cryptographic fingerprints: re-scanning a physical object under any scanner, pose, or noise threshold yields the same HASH ID. Planned applications include on-chain physical object identity registration and zero-knowledge spatial proofs.
